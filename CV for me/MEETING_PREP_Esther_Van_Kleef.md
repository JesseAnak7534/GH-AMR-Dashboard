# Meeting Preparation: Esther Van Kleef — ALARUM Project Discussion
**Date:** 2 April 2026
**Context:** Introductory meeting. Esther (University of Oxford, formerly ITM) invited you after reading your CV/cover letter forwarded by Gert Van der Auwera. She noticed your environmental AMR work and pointed you to the ALARUM project. You've read the full protocol and expressed interest.

---

## PART 1: HOW THE CONVERSATION FLOWS

The first 2–3 questions shape the entire meeting. Esther will likely open with something broad ("Tell me about yourself" or "So, Jesse, what's your story?"). Your answer here sets the frame. If you ramble or sound rehearsed, she goes into polite-but-distant mode. If you're concise, genuine, and show you think clearly, the conversation opens up.

**Golden rule:** Every answer should be 30–60 seconds. If she wants more, she'll ask. Silence after your answer is not awkward — it means she's processing.

### BLOCK E: ALARUM-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
*This is where she tests whether you actually read the paper or just skimmed the abstract.*

**Q18: "What did you take away from reading the ALARUM protocol?"**

> "Three things stood out. First, the core innovation — using pooled metagenomic data from community and environmental samples to predict resistance in clinical isolates. **This answer proves you read it carefully.** Mentioning the generalisability gap shows you think critically, not just admiringly.

**Q19: "How do you see yourself fitting into ALARUM?"**

Ghana is a neighbouring country with different ecological, sanitation, and livestock contexts — and it has established AMR infrastructure at Noguchi. A PhD project that tests whether ALARUM's predictive models generalise to Ghana would be scientifically valuable to the consortium and practically feasible given my institutional base and experience with environmental AMR frameworks there."

**This is the most important answer of the meeting.** It's concrete, it's useful to them, and it positions you as an asset, not a favour.

**Q20: "Which component of ALARUM interests you most?"**

**Q21: "What do you think the main challenges of the project are?"**

**Shows critical thinking, not just praise.**

## PART 3: QUESTIONS YOU SHOULD ASK ESTHER

*Ask 3–4 maximum. Pick based on how the conversation flows. Don't read from a list.*

### About the project:
1. **"Where is ALARUM currently in its timeline — has sampling begun, or are you still in the preparatory phase?"**
   — Shows you understand projects have phases and you want to know where you'd enter.

2. **"When the protocol mentions scaling to other West African settings, is that something with a concrete plan, or is it more of a future aspiration at this point?"**
   — Directly probes whether a Ghana expansion is realistic or hypothetical.

3. **"What has been the biggest surprise or challenge so far in the implementation?"**
   — Researchers love this question. It lets them talk about real problems, not polished summaries.

### About the PhD possibility:
4. **"If a PhD student were to join ALARUM, what would the supervision structure look like — would it be primarily through Oxford, or distributed across the consortium?"**
   — Practical and important. Shows you're thinking about how it would actually work.

5. **"Is there a specific work package or component where you feel additional capacity would be most valuable?"**
   — Lets her tell you exactly where you'd fit, rather than you guessing.

6. **"What funding mechanisms do you think would be most realistic for supporting a PhD within this framework?"**
   — Shows you're not expecting a handout. You're willing to work on the funding problem together.

### About her personally:
7. **"You moved from ITM to Oxford recently — has that changed how you're involved with ALARUM?"**
   — Shows you paid attention. Also helps you understand the supervision logistics.

## PART 4: HOW TO CLOSE THE MEETING

When the conversation naturally winds down (usually 20–30 minutes for an intro call):

> "Esther, this has been really valuable — thank you. I'm genuinely excited about the possibility of contributing to ALARUM. What would be a useful next step from my side?"

This does three things:
- Shows gratitude without being excessive
- Expresses clear interest
- Puts the ball in her court while showing you're action-oriented

**If she suggests you write a concept note or short proposal:** Say "I'd be happy to — could you point me to any specific aspect or gap you'd like me to focus on?"

**If she says she needs to discuss with the team:** Say "Of course — please take whatever time you need. I'm available whenever you'd like to follow up."

**If she's noncommittal:** Don't push. Say "Thank you again for your time. Please don't hesitate to reach out if anything comes up where I could contribute."
